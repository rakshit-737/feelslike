1 · The problem & the idea

Commercial buildings spend roughly 40–50% of their electricity on HVAC, and most of them run it deaf: a facilities team sets a fixed schedule — typically an aggressive setpoint like 22 °C from early morning to late evening — precisely because nobody can hear the occupants. Overcooling everyone, everywhere, all day is the cheapest way to avoid complaints when feedback arrives days late through a ticket system, if it arrives at all. The result is the familiar picture: sweaters and space heaters under desks in August, while the chiller runs flat out.

The feedback that could fix this already exists. People say "it's stuffy in here" out loud, in chat, constantly. It just never reaches the control loop.

FeelsLike closes that loop. A complaint typed in Slack or a web chat ("it's really stuffy in Conference Room B") is parsed into a typed, schema-validated constraint, the constraint steers a zone-level HVAC controller, and a physics-based digital twin proves the effect: the building relaxes its blanket overcooling everywhere people aren't uncomfortable, and spends energy exactly where they are.

The one-line result: over seven simulated days on identical weather, FeelsLike used 26.6% less energy than the static schedule with zero occupied minutes outside the comfort band — while the obvious alternative, simply raising the thermostat, saved slightly more (31.7%) but broke comfort for 429 minutes. Efficiency without sacrifice is the point, and both halves of that claim are measured, not asserted.

Why this is not "just a thermostat app"

  • It listens in natural language — sarcasm, typos, and Hinglish included — with a benchmarked parser and explicit anti-hallucination guardrails.
  • It arbitrates disagreement. Two people in one room asking for opposite things get a severity-weighted, time-decaying compromise with a human-readable explanation — not a war over the thermostat.
  • It proves the effect. Two identical twins run in lock-step on the same weather, one listening and one not. Every number shown is that A/B difference.
  • It degrades gracefully. No API key, no Wi-Fi, no problem: an offline rules parser keeps the entire system functional.

The contract

{"is_comfort_complaint": true,
 "zone_id": "zone_b",              // null if no known zone was named
 "issue": "too_hot" | "too_cold" | "stuffy" | "humid" | "drafty" | "other",
 "severity": 1..3,                 // 1 mild, 2 clear discomfort, 3 urgent
 "confidence": 0.0..1.0,
 "reasoning": "one short sentence"}

Guardrails that prevent judge-visible failures

  • A zone_id outside the known zone list is nulled — the system never acts on a hallucinated zone.
  • zone_id = null triggers a clarifying question, never a guess.
  • Non-thermal messages ("the projector is broken") are classified is_comfort_complaint = false and explicitly ignored.
  • Retractions ("it's fine now in Room B") clear the zone's constraints instead of filing a new complaint — including trap phrasings like "the heat issue is fixed now".
  • Every parse is Pydantic-validated; anything malformed falls back to the rules parser.

Two twins always run in lock-step on identical weather — FeelsLike vs the Static-22 °C baseline. The dashboard's racing meters, savings percentages, and comfort counters are all that live A/B difference; nothing is precomputed or staged.

3 · The digital twin: 5-zone RC thermal model

The twin is a standard lumped-parameter (resistor–capacitor) building model — the same family used in building-simulation literature — chosen so its behavior is defensible and its parameters are physically interpretable. Each zone i integrates: 

C_i · dT_i/dt = UA_i (T_out − T_i)              // envelope conduction
              + Σ_j G_ij (T_j − T_i)            // inter-zone coupling (shared walls)
              + Q_solar_i + Q_occupants_i       // gains: facade sun + 100 W/person
              + Q_hvac_i                        // cooling (negative)

HVAC is a "perfect thermostat with finite capacity": each 60 s step it removes exactly the heat needed to reach the setpoint, capped at the unit's capacity. Electrical power = thermal cooling / COP (3.4) + ventilation fan power (0 / 150 / 420 W per zone by fan level). Ventilation also adds 45 W/K of outdoor-air coupling per level — fresh air costs energy when it's hot outside, which is exactly the stuffy-vs-efficient trade-off the controller must navigate.

Weather, solar, occupancy

Weather is a seeded synthetic model of an Indian city in August: base 30 °C with a deterministic ±1.5 °C per-day wobble, a 6 °C diurnal swing (minimum ~3 AM, maximum ~3 PM), and a small smooth intra-day perturbation. Deterministic seeding is what makes the A/B race fair — every controller sees byte-identical weather. Solar gain follows each facade's orientation (east peaks 9:00, south 12:30, west 16:00; north gets diffuse only). Occupancy follows weekday/weekend piecewise schedules per zone — the lunch surge in the cafeteria and the 14:00 conference meeting are the recurring events the comfort-memory feature learns. An Open-Meteo hook exists to swap in real hourly weather with no interface change.

Comfort accounting

The occupied comfort band is 23.0–26.5 °C (ASHRAE-style for offices). Every simulated minute a zone is occupied and outside the band counts as a violation minute, with degree-minutes tracked above and below separately. Unoccupied zones accrue nothing — comfort is a promise to people, not to empty rooms. Energy is priced at ₹9/kWh (commercial ToU average) and 0.71 kg CO₂/kWh (India grid, CEA ~FY23).

4 · Understanding complaints: the NLP parser

The parser's job is deceptively narrow — one chat message in, one JSON constraint out — but the input space is human: sarcasm ("great, another sauna day 🙃"), Hinglish ("cabin mein bahut garmi hai bhai"), typos, indirect phrasing ("I'm wearing a jacket"), and messages that merely mention a room without complaining about it.

Two parsers, one interface

Primary — LLM structured extraction. A system prompt carries the schema, the known zone list with aliases, explicit anti-hallucination rules, and worked examples (including Hinglish and the projector-is-broken negative). Any OpenAI-compatible provider works via LLM_BASE_URL: Anthropic Claude, OpenAI, or free tiers — Groq (Llama 3.3 70B, sub-second), Google AI Studio (Gemini 2.5 Flash), or a fully local Ollama model. The demo runs on Groq's free tier at zero cost.

Fallback — deterministic rules. Keyword-cascade parser: most-specific issue first (drafty before cold, so "cold air blowing on me" is a draft, not a temperature complaint), zone resolution via aliases plus "room B"-style regex, severity from intensity words. It is instant, offline, and free — the demo-day insurance. The switch is automatic: any LLM error, timeout, or missing key degrades gracefully, and the UI labels every message with its parse source (llm or rules) and latency honestly.

Retraction handling

"It's fine now, thanks" must cancel a constraint, not file one — and "the heat issue in the lobby is fixed now" must not be read as a heat complaint just because it contains the word "heat". A retraction detector (all-clear phrases, guarded by ongoing-markers like "but" / "still") runs before keyword matching; on retraction with a known zone, the zone's active constraints are expired immediately and the feed shows an all-clear. This was a known failure mode we designed for rather than disclosed away.

Latency

Rules: ~0 ms. Groq free tier: typically 0.3–0.8 s. Claude/OpenAI: 1–2 s. The complaint → visible action loop on the dashboard is under a second in the offline path and 1–2 s end-to-end with a hosted LLM — fast enough that the parse feels like a reflex on stage.

5 · The constraint engine: decay, arbitration, memory

Complaints decay — recency-weighted democracy, not a ticket queue

A complaint's influence decays exponentially with a 45-minute half-life and expires at 2 hours unless re-reported: 

decay(t)  = 0.5 ^ (age / 45 min)          (0 after 2 h)
weight(t) = severity × confidence × decay(t)

This single design choice solves three problems at once: stale complaints don't haunt the building forever, a persistent problem naturally re-asserts itself (people complain again), and no manual "close ticket" step is ever needed.

Conflict arbitration

Opposing constraints in one zone resolve by weighted mean — weight = severity × confidence × decay — with a human-readable explanation generated for the dashboard and the Slack reply: "CONFLICT in zone: stuffy (sev 2, 3m ago) vs too cold (sev 2, 1m ago) → severity-weighted compromise: −0.1 °C." Neither party wins forever, because both claims decay. Every office has this argument; FeelsLike settles it transparently in seconds.

Comfort memory (learning the building's habits)

The store keeps complaint history, and a pattern miner clusters it by (zone, issue, hour-of-day). When the same complaint has occurred on ≥ 2 distinct days at a similar hour (±1 h), the system pre-applies a gentle constraint (severity 1, confidence 0.5) 30 minutes before the pattern's usual time and announces it in the feed: "Conference Room B usually reports stuffy around 14:00 — pre-applying a gentle fix." The building stops waiting to be told twice. Memory only learns from real occupant complaints, never from its own pre-applies, so it cannot self-reinforce.

8 · NLP benchmark: methodology & honest numbers

The benchmark is 70 labeled cases scoring three things per message: complaint detection (is this a comfort complaint at all?), zone extraction, and issue extraction. "Exact triple" requires all three correct.

The split is the methodology. The 30-case dev set is what the rules parser was tuned on — its score there is table stakes, not evidence. The 40-case held-out set was written after the rules were frozen: typos ("its friezing in the confrence room"), sarcasm ("love how I need gloves to type"), Hinglish variants, multi-zone messages, retractions with keyword traps, and negatives designed to fool keyword matching ("the coffee machine is steaming hot again"). Those cases were later debugged against, which is what retired them as a generalization measure — see the blind probe below.

The blind probe: what the parser scores on language it has never seen

A held-out split stops being held out the moment someone debugs against it. Ours did: after a parser rewrite the held-out exact-triple score read 98%, which measures how thoroughly those specific sentences were fixed, not how well the parser generalizes. So we keep a second set, evals/blind_probe.json, that is never used for tuning — if a case in it ever informs a fix, that case is retired and replaced.

Why publish the lower number? Because the difference between the tuned split's 98% and the probe's 80% is the finding. It quantifies how much of a held-out score survives contact with genuinely new phrasing, and it is the only NLP number this report quotes as generalization.

What still fails (blind-probe cases, quoted as measured)

  • "whoever runs the cafeteria AC hates warm-blooded people" — sarcasm-inverted; missed det, issue.
  • "the microwave in the pantry is red hot" — appliance-negative; missed zoneset.
  • "could someone look at the temperature situation" — ambiguous-needs-clarify; missed det.
  • "printer room is fine but the open office is unbearable" — contrast-two-clauses; missed det, issue.
The rules path is not just insurance. On 20 unseen cases the staged rules parser scores 80% exact triple against the hosted LLM's 60%, leading on 3 of 4 axes and trailing on 0. The runtime still prefers the LLM whenever an API key is present (rules are the no-key, no-Wi-Fi path), so this result is a live argument against that default rather than a description of it: on unseen phrasing the component we wrote generalizes better than the model we called, and the probe is what makes that visible instead of assumed.

9 · The live demo system

Dashboard

A single-file, zero-dependency dashboard (inline CSS/JS, no build step, nothing to break offline) polling /api/state at 1 Hz:

  • The race — paired racing bars (FeelsLike vs baseline kWh) with the live saved-% as the headline, and the comfort-violations counter beside it: both halves of the claim on one screen.
  • Floor plan — the real 5-zone geometry, color-mapped to temperature, with setpoints, occupancy, fan levels, complaint offsets, pulsing constraint markers, and an amber conflict ring when a zone is under arbitration. Unoccupied zones render dimmed so overnight drift doesn't read as failure.
  • Occupant channel — chat with quick-chips that are the demo script (the conflict pair, Hinglish, and a deliberate non-complaint), each message showing its parse, source badge, and latency.
  • Resilience UX — typed messages survive failed sends; a reconnecting pill appears if the backend dies; the parser badge flips to rules (offline fallback) honestly if the LLM path fails mid-demo.

Slack / Teams

POST /api/slack accepts Slack slash-command form posts and Teams-style JSON. A complaint typed in Slack changes a zone on the dashboard in seconds, and the bot replies in-channel with the parsed action and explanation. The web chat is the same endpoint — the fallback if venue Wi-Fi dislikes webhooks.

10 · Scale story: money & carbon

The simulated building is ~520 m². Its measured week — 192 kWh saved, ₹1,729, 136 kg CO₂ — is small on purpose: it's one honest building. The point is the mechanism scales linearly with floor area and requires no new hardware: complaints already come from tools offices use, and the control outputs are ordinary setpoint/fan commands any BMS accepts.

11 · Limitations & path to production

Known limitations (disclosed, not discovered)

  • "In simulation." The zero-violation result holds because HVAC sizing is adequate in the model. Real buildings have undersized units and thermal surprises; the claim on stage is always "in simulation".
  • Parser ceiling on unseen phrasing. 80% exact-triple on the 20-case blind probe (§8); complaint detection (85%) fails before zone extraction (95%) does. Benchmarked and shown, not estimated.
  • Multi-zone complaints. A message naming two rooms still ends up as a single-zone constraint downstream; disclosed rather than worked around.
  • RL not yet shipping. The PPO agent trades 2 viol-min for 2.8 kWh/day; below our bar (§7).
  • Synthetic weather/occupancy. Deterministic by design for fair A/B; the Open-Meteo hook and BMS-log calibration are the path to real data.

Path to production

  • Same architecture, real actuators: the twin swaps for BACnet/Modbus writes; the constraint engine and parser are unchanged.
  • Calibration: fit each zone's R/C parameters from a week of BMS logs — a standard system-identification step for lumped-parameter models.
  • Privacy by construction: constraints aggregate at zone level; no identity is needed or stored beyond a display name in the feed.
  • Safety envelope: setpoints are clamped to 21.5–29 °C and offsets cap at ±1.8 °C — the building can be steered, never hijacked.
  • RL flip-in: the PPO policy is a drop-in controller class; when a longer run clears the comfort bar, one line switches the demo to it.

12 · Anticipated questions

QuestionAnswer
How do you stop the LLM hallucinating a setpoint?It never emits one. The LLM only classifies into a schema; unknown zones are nulled, low confidence asks a clarifying question, non-complaints are ignored. Control math is deterministic code.
Is the RL real?2M-step PPO with learning curves and a 4-way ablation — and we're showing you the run where it didn't beat our bar, which is how you know the numbers are real.
Two people disagree?Live demo: severity × confidence × recency weighted compromise with a printed explanation. It decays, so neither wins forever.
How real is the twin?Standard lumped-parameter RC model (literature-backed) with zone coupling, solar, occupancy, and capacity-limited HVAC. Calibration path: fit R/C from a week of BMS logs.
What if the Wi-Fi dies on stage?It can — the parser auto-falls back to offline rules and the badge says so. The demo was designed to survive its own failure modes.
Why not just raise the thermostat?We benchmarked exactly that (Reactive 24 °C): more savings, 429 minutes of broken comfort. That trade-off is the product's reason to exist.
Privacy?Zone-level aggregation; no identity required.
